How one glucose reading moves through diaswarm

From an AAPS database row to a follower's screen: the record format, the encryption, the logs, the network, where the bytes are stored, and what each party can and can't see.

This shows the path the phones run today: diaswarm-keys over diaswarm-net. The older diaswarm-core vault with per-reader wraps is still in the repo as a reference, and some docs still describe it. The custody layer has not been independently reviewed. See SECURITY.md.

Latency and availability

Two questions people who run Nightscout ask first: how old is the number when a follower sees it, and what happens when the phone that has the data is unreachable. Here is what the code does, what has been measured, and what hasn't.

Subject
The plugin seals and publishes every 60 s, or sooner once 32 KB of backfill is waiting (SHADOW_SEAL_INTERVAL_MS).
Holders
Pushed to over gossip as each entry is appended. There is no polling interval on this hop.
Reader
Polls every 120 s while awake. Measured at 105 s and 123 s between unattended refreshes.
Measured
2026-09-11: subject on mobile data behind CGNAT, reader on home wifi. Each refresh showed the newest reading as 0m old, and the screen read 9.4 mmol/L, 63 seconds old.

The reader isn't pushed to, by design. The subject doesn't know who follows them: the grant log names nobody, and a fetch doesn't say who is fetching. So the reader has to ask (D17).

In a pocket, Android sets the interval. Doze batches background work, so a reader phone that isn't excused from battery optimisation refreshes much less often. A held-open subscription would help while the app is open, but Doze closes sockets in the background either way. That's why every reading is drawn with its age, and counts as stale at 15 minutes.

When something is unreachable

What a reader gets in each case. The tags say how far each claim has been tested.

shown on phones tested off-device known gap

The subject's phone is off or out of signal

Readers fetch from the three holders, which verify and serve identical ciphertext. Nothing new arrives until the phone is back.

shown on phones with the reference vault: a stranger peer served a 73-day, 33,800-record history while the subject's phone was off.
tested off-device with the shipped vault: two peers in one process. Not yet repeated on phones (D28).

A holder leaves the pool

Every peer recomputes the pool size, bucket depth and top three holders on its next pass. Survivors pick up the gap. There's no repair process and nothing to elect.

tested off-device Disjoint shares need four or more devices. The pool has only ever been three phones.

Every holder is a phone, and they're all asleep

Doze, and Android killing the foreground service after about six hours, can leave no holder serving. A desktop peer is mains-powered and has neither problem, so one in the pool removes this case.

known gap on phones alone. The desktop peer (diaswarm-peer) is released for this.

The pool is smaller than three

With fewer than three peers, every peer holds every bucket, so there are as many copies as there are peers.

tested off-device depth_for(2) is 1, so both peers hold both buckets on every run.

The relay is unreachable

Phones on the same network still find each other over mDNS. Across networks, phones behind NAT can't find each other without a relay. It's the single third party in the design. The relay URL travels in the invite, so you can run your own.

shown on phones through n0's relay across CGNAT; LAN-only over mDNS.

The reader's phone has been quiet for hours

Ayni shows every reading's age. It tells "held but can't open" apart from "nothing sent" and reports which peers it couldn't reach. Nothing yet warns that the silence has gone on too long.

known gap No alert for long silence. Stale readings never raise glucose alerts.
